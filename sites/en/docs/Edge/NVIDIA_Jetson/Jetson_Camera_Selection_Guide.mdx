# Jetson Camera Selection Guide

Compare cameras for a reComputer or reServer by interface, resolution, field of view and the board each one is validated on. The selector below filters the same 29 models; the table further down lists them as plain text, so every model and guide link is readable without opening a dialog.

## Pick the interface first

CSI, USB and GMSL2 solve different problems, and the interface usually decides the rest.

**CSI (MIPI CSI-2).** A ribbon cable from the camera to the carrier board, no adapter and no hub. On J30/J40 boards this is the cheapest way to add a camera, but the ribbon has to match both ends and the Seeed BSP has to carry the sensor driver for your JetPack version.

**USB.** Works on any reComputer with a free port and needs no BSP work. The trade-offs sit on the host side: all USB cameras on one controller share bandwidth and power, and a 4K stream leaves little room for a second camera.

**GMSL2.** One coax carries data and power, so the camera can sit metres away from the board with a locking FAKRA connector. It needs the matching GMSL extension board and the 3G or 6G overlay for the sensor; the same link also carries the depth cameras in the Sensing and Orbbec families.

## Which board reaches which camera

| Interface | What the board needs | Typical host |
|---|---|---|
| CSI | Ribbon to the carrier; matching BSP | J30/J40 · Orin Nano/NX |
| USB | A free port; check power budget | Any reComputer or reServer |
| GMSL2 | GMSL extension board + 3G/6G overlay | Robotics J401, J501, J501 Mini, J601; Industrial J501 |
| GMSL2 depth | Same as GMSL2 | Robotics J401, J501, J601 |

## Lens and field of view

Field of view comes in two shapes and they are not interchangeable. Fixed-lens CSI modules quote a diagonal angle, which is why the IMX219 series carries the number in the model name — 77°, 130°, 160°, 200°. Depth cameras quote horizontal × vertical, because the depth stream has its own aspect ratio.

Where the mount is replaceable (M12, CS, the HQ camera), the lens decides the angle and the number in the table is only a starting point. A 196° GMSL2 lens is for surround coverage; it will not read a label across the yard. Wide always costs pixels per object, so pick the narrowest lens that still keeps the target in frame.

## Bandwidth and frame rate

CSI lanes are dedicated, so two CSI cameras do not compete. USB does: all cameras behind one controller split the same bus, and two 1080p30 streams are usually fine where one 4K stream plus a second camera is not. GMSL2 gives each camera its own link, which is why an eight-camera build is a GMSL2 design rather than a USB one.

Frame rate in the table is the sensor's listed maximum, not a promise for your pipeline. TensorRT inference, colour conversion and recording all take time per frame; measure the end-to-end rate on the target before sizing a build.

## Common traps

- A ribbon that fits the camera but not the carrier — check both connectors before ordering.
- GMSL2 without the overlay: the camera enumerates but no stream appears.
- USB cameras on a hub port that cannot supply the current; the device drops out under load.
- Depth range is not accuracy. A camera rated to 10 m is not reliable at 10 m for a distance alarm; set thresholds from a field test.
- SKU suffixes change the lens and the serializer. The family name in a support list does not guarantee the exact variant you ordered.
