8. Building the Workspace

The task is always the same: put three vials into the vial rack. This chapter builds the studio that makes the task recordable and repeatable — hardware list, lightbox, brackets, prop positions, and cameras.

HardwareLightboxBracketsProp positionsCamerasChecklist

8.1 Hardware List​

Bill of materials

8.1 Hardware List

ItemDescriptionQuantity
SO-ARM101 Pro kit (Leader + Follower)SO-ARM101 Low-Cost AI Arm Kit Pro1
USB cameras ×2One for the wrist mount, one external; any UVC camera at 640×480 and 30 FPS2
Camera mountsWorkshop wrist bracket + a rigid external stand2

Non-reflective matTable surface the props sit on1

Policies learn whatever the cameras see. A plain desk with a window behind it produces a dataset full of lighting changes; the studio removes that noise. The approach follows the beginner course's dataset chapters (Beginner Chapter 11 — Task Design and Chapter 12 — Camera Configuration).

8.2 The Lightbox​

摄影棚

8.2 The Lightbox

The minimum studio:

ItemPurpose
Foam-board box or plain matUniform background behind the props
Diffuse white light (a lamp with a shade, or two LEDs at 45°)Soft, stable lighting without hard shadows
Non-reflective table surfaceVials and rack stay visible from both cameras
Fixed robot positionThe follower bolted or taped in place, base facing the props
One light source beats three

Multiple uneven light sources create moving highlights on glossy vials. One diffuse overhead light plus the room's ambient light is easier to replicate on a different day — which matters when you record more episodes later.

8.3 Brackets and Mounts​

支架

8.3 Brackets and Mounts

Two mounts carry the cameras; both must be rigid enough to survive a week of recording without drifting.

Wrist camera bracket. Mounted on the follower's gripper, following the official SO-101 workshop camera bracket: the camera sits above the jaws, tilted down about 45°, so the gripper tips and the grasped object both stay in frame through the whole reach. Print or buy the workshop bracket and tighten the screws firmly — a wrist camera that rotates mid-episode invalidates every episode after it.

External camera stand. Any rigid stand works: a small tripod, a 2020 extrusion post, or an articulated arm clamped to the table. Position it above and in front of the workspace so one frame covers the rack, all three vial positions, and most of the follower's reach. Do not hand-hold it and do not clamp it to the same board the follower pushes against.

Cable routing: leave slack at the wrist so the loop does not snag, and route both camera cables away from the follower's workspace.

8.4 Prop Positions​

物料位置

8.4 Prop Positions

The task definition is part of the dataset. Keep it consistent:

  • Vial rack: to the right of the follower's base, inside comfortable reach — the same side and distance as the digital twin;
  • Three vials: scattered in the work area in front of the arm, roughly 10–20 cm out; re-place them by hand between episodes;
  • Task sentence: Put the three vials into the vial rack — the exact string used in Chapter 9 recording and in the digital twin's dataset. Do not paraphrase it between episodes.
Measure once, then build a fixture

Tape the rack position and mark the vial area on the mat. Reproducible positions matter more than millimeter accuracy.

If you also plan to collect simulated episodes (Chapter 12), match the bench to the twin's scene — the twin defines the specification:

CheckSim referenceReal bench
Rack positionFixed in SO101_RACK_POSITIONSame side and distance relative to the base
Vial start areaSO101_VIAL_POSITIONSSame 10–20 cm region in front of the arm
Camera viewsWrist + externalSame framing, 640×480, 30 FPS
LightingDome + key lightDiffuse, no strong shadow across the rack

A 1–2 cm mismatch is tolerable. A rack on the opposite side, a camera at a different height, or a hand-held camera is not.

8.5 Cameras​

Two views

8.5 Cameras

List the cameras and capture a preview of each:

lerobot-find-cameras opencv

Preview images are written to outputs/captured_images/; the file name contains the device index, for example opencv__dev_video4.png means index 4. Export the indices:

export CAMERA_GRIPPER=4
export CAMERA_EXTERNAL=6

Indices can change after a reboot or a replug, so repeat this check before every recording session. Lock the focus and exposure if the camera supports it — autofocus hunting during an episode shows up as blurry training frames.

Verify both views during teleoperation:

lerobot-teleoperate \
  --robot.type=so101_follower \
  --robot.port=$ROBOT_PORT \
  --robot.id=$ROBOT_ID \
  --teleop.type=so101_leader \
  --teleop.port=$TELEOP_PORT \
  --teleop.id=$TELEOP_ID \
  --display_data=true \
  --robot.cameras="{
    front: {type: opencv, index_or_path: $CAMERA_GRIPPER, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30},
    external: {type: opencv, index_or_path: $CAMERA_EXTERNAL, width: 640, height: 480, fps: 30}
  }"
Camera names are part of the dataset schema

This course records with the keys front (wrist camera) and external (overhead camera) so the real dataset has exactly the same feature names as the simulated dataset in Chapter 12. That is what makes sim-plus-real co-training possible without renaming features. Chapter 10 maps these two streams to GR00T's wrist and front inputs.

8.6 Studio Checklist​

Before Chapter 9

8.6 Studio Checklist

  • Lightbox or mat in place, one diffuse light, no hard shadows on the rack;
  • Follower fixed in position; leader within comfortable reach;
  • Wrist bracket rigid, camera tilted about 45°, sees the jaws;
  • External camera on a rigid stand, sees the rack and all three vial slots;
  • Both cameras preview at 640×480 and 30 FPS, focus locked;
  • CAMERA_GRIPPER and CAMERA_EXTERNAL exported;
  • Rack taped in place, vial area marked, task sentence fixed.
